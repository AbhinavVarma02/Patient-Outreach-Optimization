# Haryana analysis protocol

This retrospective specification was written before the new sensitivity and policy runs. The published Figure 4 results and the archive's arm support were already known. No outcome-based choice of split, tuning parameters or comparator is planned.

## Replication and sensitivity

Reproduce the published additive ten-indicator regression in the first-implementation ambassador sample. Report the population-weighted pure control mean separately from the adjusted information-hub coefficient. Compute 95% t intervals using the authors' sub-center CR0 convention and reproduce the plot using its code path. The authors' script multiplies the stored covariance after fitting, then reads the original precomputed confidence limits. Compare sub-center CR0 with primary health center (PHC) CR1, equal village-month weights with PHC CR1, and a common April–October calendar restriction with PHC CR1. The sensitivity intervals are pointwise. If reporting the ten arm tests jointly, apply Holm's familywise adjustment to the PHC-clustered p-values. Compare at least the primary coefficient and covariance against statsmodels. Neither a robust covariance nor the calendar restriction identifies effects under arbitrary cross-PHC interference.

## Community policy experiment

Restrict to information-hub and no-ambassador villages in sub-centers with both assignments, holding other randomized domains fixed within each sub-center. The outcome is mean recorded measles-1 vaccinations per observed village-month in the stated implementation window, with equal village weights. Conditional on the observed matched arm counts, within-block exchangeability and assignment-independent outcome observation, the two-arm marginal probability is the treated share within that block by design. This conditional argument does not provide unconditional village-level propensities for the full design. In the absence of interference between villages, the estimand compares allocation policies at the same expected fraction of eligible villages assigned an ambassador. Diffusion inside a village is part of the assigned intervention. Neighboring-village diffusion can make the comparison only a sensitivity under the trial's assignment mix.

Use a deterministic SHA-256 ordering of PHCs, seed 20261124, to allocate two fifths to training and the rest to held-out evaluation. All villages and months from each PHC belong to a single partition. Use only baseline vaccination card, ever-measles and literacy shares. Validate unique village-month keys, assignment and nesting, baseline join cardinality, fixed feature ranges and both arms in each retained block. Audit observation windows and exclude records with irregular feature values by failing validation. Keep raw records, identifiers, split membership and fitted model parameters in ignored storage. Require at least 11 whole PHCs per partition and 11 villages in each assignment arm in the holdout before reporting policy results.

Compare four fixed policies at 50% expected village capacity. Uniform is the primary reference. Low baseline measles and low predicted control count are strong, interpretable comparators. The learned score uses ridge T-learner predictions of the difference between arms. Use arm-specific ridge models with training-only standardization and fixed penalty 10. Estimate paired policy contrasts using the held-out block-propensity augmented scores with train-only outcome fits. On the test covariates use the top half of the score distribution, fractionally allocating ties. The quota is conditional on the evaluation roster and uses no outcomes. Compare against unaugmented scores and a common April–October outcome window as sensitivity, with the policy and nuisance fits frozen. Show PHC-clustered t intervals and Bonferroni simultaneous intervals for the three primary comparisons. Use synthetic known-answer tests for the score, capacity and interval aggregation. This small, internal geographic holdout is exploratory. Report support, uncertainty and whether any inference survives simultaneous correction. Do not promote a tuned winner, infer individual effects, describe the split as temporal validation or infer a saturation or incentive policy from the ambassador-only contrast.
