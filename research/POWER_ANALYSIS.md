# Power analysis

The supplied oracle script was rerun unchanged using NumPy 2.3.5. At 10% coverage and 2× effect concentration, it estimated power of 13% at N=10,200 and 79% at N=120,000. Its scaling estimate for 80% power was 126,070. The 3× alternative gave about 30,622 records. These calculations concern retrospective evaluation with perfect ranking, simulated standard errors and random outreach as the comparator. [Actual run](reports/original_oracle_reproduction.txt)

The public pilot is a different design. It compares two frozen policies prospectively. If outreach capacity is b and the average per-contact effect is t, uniform outreach adds b × t to baseline. The model assigns effect k × t to the top b share and sets the remaining effect to preserve the average. A frozen ranker's capture fraction scales its gap from uniform to the oracle. The expected policy difference is b × capture × (k × t − t) while capacity is below 100%. The variance uses the two binomial rates and allocation proportions. Enrollment inflates for attrition and the approximate design effect 1 + (mean cluster size − 1) × ICC. The minimum detectable policy difference holds variance fixed.

At 5,000 simulated draws per scenario, the two approximately 80%-powered examples achieved 80.14% for balanced allocation and 81.02% for 3:1 allocation. The null simulated rejection rate was 4.94%. All three analytic calculations fell inside their Wilson simulation intervals. The stated clustering and attrition sensitivities were not covered by this particular simulation. The browser default and independent Python implementation both calculate 544,545 enrolled records under the app's illustrative assumptions. [Validation run](reports/pilot_validation.json)
