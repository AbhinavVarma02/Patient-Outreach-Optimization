# Literature review

Risk predicts the baseline outcome. Benefit is the difference in outcomes under alternative interventions. A strong risk model can align with intervention benefit when baseline risk and effect co-move. Even good estimates of baseline risk cannot resolve effect heterogeneity without experimental contrasts or defendable identification assumptions.

Rosenbaum and colleagues tested risk disclosure and message framing across three trials. Their modality trial, reported in the supplement, varied assigned bundles within eligibility strata. Study 4's primary population could receive all three channels. The reported letter estimate does not identify effects in patients who lack some contact methods. A same-season contrast across Studies 3 and 4 changes the population and intervention and therefore cannot isolate clinical-risk-band modification.

Imai and Li formalize evaluation of learned individualized rules, including resource constraints. The current toolkit supports the narrower paired contrast between independently fixed policies. Yadlowsky and colleagues formalize ranking metrics, which are useful alongside fixed-budget value once independent evaluation is feasible. Shchetkina's vaccination analysis and Bastani et al.'s inference-aware personalization show direct precedent and motivate independent policy selection. Athey et al. compare clinical or outcome-risk analogues with benefit targeting in a different application. The Medicaid article is adjacent observational evidence.
