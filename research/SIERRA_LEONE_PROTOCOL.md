# Sierra Leone replication and village policy protocol

This retrospective protocol uses the reported Nature estimates, the metadata and codebook, and an assignment and missingness audit. The authors' published results were known. The new policy scores and holdout performance had not been computed at freeze. [Article and Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07158-w) · [Replication files](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/PRXF5Z)

## Replication

Use the original Stata bytes for the 150-community and 31,913-person files. Verify sizes and SHA-256 against the deposit inventory. Use the individual codebook, the Stata labels and the author script as text. Reproduce Extended Data Table 3 columns 1 and 3 and Extended Data Table 1 columns 1 and 3. Include census-enumerated adults (`above18 == 1`, minimum observed age 18). Use formatted point and standard-error equality at three decimals. The person-level access model absorbs triplet and clusters by village. The equal-village model averages the adult endpoint within village and uses triplet fixed effects with the author's residual-based OLS variance. The component arm model fits both mobile packages against control. The extra visit model restricts to door-to-door villages and `periphery == 0`, absorbs village and clusters by residential structure, the randomized unit. Report the conventional t intervals under the corresponding variance convention. The authors' `boottest` p-values and adjusted columns are outside this replication gate. Verify point estimates and uncorrected sandwich covariance against statsmodels; apply the author's finite-sample factor separately.

## Randomization and population

Each triplet assigns one village to each arm, so the marginal probability of receiving either mobile package is 2/3. Mobile package assignment includes a clinic, community mobilization and one of two additional mobilization strategies. The pooled estimand averages these two versions with equal probability. Door-to-door versus small group compares two bundles. The incremental visit lottery assigns residential structures, which can contain multiple households. The published adult regression weights residents, with the identifying contrast induced by within-village assignment variation. Do not use visit receipt as treatment. For a future structure-level policy, require a documented preassignment roster and lottery denominator for every included village, positive probability in both arms, and a credible fixed-saturation interference estimand. The cap of 20 does not reconstruct the lottery from the released data in every village. Hold this policy comparison pending reconciliation. Publish no household or person rankings.

The community endpoint is the released adult vaccination rate at endline. Control has no equivalent endline survey flag. Report source ascertainment, coverage and the effect of retaining the two implementation failures. Non-census clinic attendees cannot enter the rate denominator. The trial reported outside-village attendance and possible within-village spillover. The new policy value assumes no cross-village interference and consistency of the equal mixture of package versions; any interference threatens transport to a different allocation pattern.

## Fixed village policy comparison

Use equal village weights. Allocate 30 whole triplets to training and 20 to evaluation by increasing SHA-256 digest of `20261124:` followed by the decimal triplet identifier. Freeze a contract with 2015 census locality immunization share, literacy share, mean age and share beyond five miles of a clinic, plus locally enumerated pretreatment adult vaccination share. Complete finite values are required. Never use identifiers, treatment, receipt, attendance, endline variables, costs or survey participation as predictors. Training uses the whole training roster only. Fit ridge regressions by randomized arm for control vs pooled access using training means and population standard deviations, unpenalized intercept and penalty 10 on standardized slopes. Clip arm predictions to [0,1]. Also fit a ridge DR-learner with penalty 10 to training augmented scores using five deterministic triplet folds assigned in hash order. This is an exploratory comparison of two low-complexity models suited to a small trial. Use no holdout tuning.

All policies assign the same expected 50% of eligible villages, with tie fractions and ranking computed from each held-out covariate roster. Compare uniform with low baseline uptake, low predicted control uptake, ridge T-learner benefit and ridge DR-learner benefit. Hold nuisance fits fixed. The paired primary score is `(policy probability − 0.5) × AIPW treatment contrast` with known propensity 2/3. Also report an unaugmented estimate. Compare the pooled effect in both halves of the baseline-uptake rank using a predeclared interaction contrast, split by fractional ties. This is the fifth contrast in the simultaneous family. No selection follows these assessments.

Report independent-triplet nominal t intervals and conservative two-sided Hoeffding bounds across the five contrasts at familywise 95%. For the bound, enumerate the three legal pooled assignments per triplet and use the extrema of scores over binary outcome-rate endpoints. Hoeffding applies conditional on trained rules and the held-out roster if triplet lotteries are independent and potential outcomes bounded, with no cross-village interference. The same population and interval convention apply when validating in known-truth simulations. Report calibration over fixed rosters, null and heterogeneous effects, and separate spillover diagnostics. Corroborate the score by enumerating the assignment distribution in known-answer fixtures. Findings are internal geographic evaluation. No temporal, patient-specific, operational or causal transport claim follows from this experiment.
